What I'm thankful for in my career in 2026.

I was inspired by a podcast [last year](/blog/career-gratitude-in-2025) to start a tradition of reflection and gratitude, and I intend to keep it.

_(That same podcast is also keeping up its tradition: [19 Reasons to Be Thankful for Video \[Games\] (2026 Edition)](https://www.patreon.com/PostGames/posts/19-reasons-to-be-175166695).)_

It has been a tough year. Agentic coding finally got good with Claude Opus 4.5, which brought with it new pressures for software engineers. Most people I know are being asked to do more with less while their purchasing power continues to diminish. The hobby communities I participate in universally despise LLMs over valid environmental, creative theft, and job security concerns. But it feels like an existential threat to my own career and family's livelihood if I don't lean into it.

That said, there were still positives in 2026.

## Supportive family

This year, like all years, I'm thankful for a family who supports the long and odd hours sometimes required by my career. Especially when [pager alerts](/blog/your-service-should-make-a-sound-when-it-flatlines) go off in the [middle of the night](/blog/if-it-doesnt-page-me-im-not-looking-at-it).

[Igir](https://igir.io/) saw another major release in 2026 (v5.0.0) and [+282 GitHub stars](https://emanuelef.github.io/daily-stars-explorer/#/emmercm/igir?start=1767254400000&end=1795680000000&transformation=none). It takes a substantial amount of my free time to maintain, and I'm thankful for a life and a family that allow me so much time for hobbies.

## Gainful employment

In 2026, I'm happy to just be employed. I'm constantly hearing from friends that their companies are having layoffs, even multiple times in one year. I follow business news in the video game industry, and it has been an awful time for creatives ([Epic Games](https://insider-gaming.com/epic-games-1000-employees-mass-layoffs/), [Bungie](https://insider-gaming.com/bungie-announces-layoffs-following-the-end-of-destiny-2/), [Xbox Game Studios and its divestments](https://insider-gaming.com/xbox-big-reset/), [id](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/report-around-half-of-the-id-software-team-have-been-laid-off), [BioWare](https://blog.bioware.com/2026/10/21/bioware-update/), [Ubisoft](https://insider-gaming.com/ubisoft-closes-halifax-studio-employees-unionized/) [multiple](https://insider-gaming.com/ubisoft-closes-belgrade-studio-all-employees-let-go/) [times](https://insider-gaming.com/ubisoft-laying-off-166-people/), and so many more).

I had a lot of changes in my personal life in 2026 where I traveled or loved ones traveled to me. I'm grateful that employment allowed me to share so many special moments with so many people.

## Years of experience

Reflecting on how the software engineering industry has changed, I'm grateful to have so many years of experience under my belt before agentic coding increased the demands on everyone. I don't think technical ability, leadership, and maturity are guaranteed outcomes from years of experience, but it sure can help. I'm grateful for years of experience teaching me (often the hard way) what is good/viable/effective, bad/nonviable/ineffective, and everything in between. Agentic coding can speed up some tasks, but it doesn't matter if you miss an important turn and crash into the wall.

I'm not replaceable, at least not yet.

## Open-source software & maintainers

Modern software is built on the backs of people who maintain critical components out of a sense of interest, passion, duty, goodwill, or some other noun that typically doesn't pay the bills ([xkcd: Dependency](https://xkcd.com/2347/)). This website has [76 non-dev dependencies](https://github.com/emmercm/www/blob/143718cd8a0a0d8959f15cf556c5b888dd5d988e/package.json#L22-L97), and [Igir](https://igir.io/) has [29](https://github.com/emmercm/igir/blob/v5.8.1/package.json#L76-L104). My industry wouldn't have gotten this far without well-maintained open-source software.

The pressure on these unthanked maintainers has never been higher. Popular repositories are seeing a rate of agent-assisted bug reports and pull requests that no human can stay on top of. And the rate of high-severity vulnerabilities being reported every week has every security team reeling ([PHP2Shell](https://www.php2shell.com/), [SSHole](https://sshhole.com/), [Java4Shell](https://calif.io/java4shell)).

This year, I'm thankful for everyone who maintains or responsibly participates in the open-source software ecosystem.

## Partners in my leadership chain

I mentioned this last year, and even though my leadership chain completely changed this year, I still think it's true. I have a strong relationship with my engineering leaders, and it cuts both ways. It makes my daily life so much better than it could be. I trust that my leaders are steering our organization in the right direction and toward the right outcomes, and they trust that I make long-term beneficial decisions and appropriate trade-offs.
